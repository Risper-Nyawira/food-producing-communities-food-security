# Conclusions and Recommendations

## Conclusion

This project finds that national food production and household food security are related but not interchangeable. Kenya produced maize every year in the analysis period, and output reached more than 4 million tonnes in 2023. However, the prevalence of moderate or severe food insecurity increased from 50.7% in the 2014-2016 rolling period to 72.8% in the 2021-2023 rolling period.

The result is consistent with a food-access problem as well as a food-availability problem. Food-producing households may sell crops to meet cash needs and later face high retail prices. Production also changes with yield, harvested area, rainfall, heat, and drought conditions. Price series coverage begins later than production coverage, so it should be interpreted as supporting context rather than a complete explanation for every year.

The evidence therefore supports a cautious answer to the research question: food-producing communities can still be food insecure when production does not translate into affordable, stable, and accessible food for households. National averages cannot show which counties or households are most affected, and the three-year food-security indicator should not be treated as a single-year outcome.

## Recommendations

1. Improve household access to food, not only production. During price or climate shocks, target cash transfers, school-feeding programmes, and social protection to vulnerable households.
2. Strengthen climate resilience through drought-tolerant seed, irrigation where viable, extension services, soil management, and timely climate information for farmers.
3. Reduce post-harvest losses with better storage, drying, aggregation, and access to warehouse or market information systems.
4. Improve market access and price transparency. Farmers need reliable local buyers and price information, while consumers need protection from extreme price spikes.
5. Extend the analysis to county level. Add IPC or FEWS NET classifications, county poverty or income data, and household consumption data to identify the communities behind national trends.
6. Build an early-warning dashboard combining climate, production, prices, and food-security indicators so interventions can begin before a severe deterioration.

## How to present this in your portfolio

State the conclusion as an evidence-based interpretation, not a causal claim. For example: "The analysis shows that maize production alone did not coincide with improving food-security outcomes. This suggests that affordability, climate resilience, income, and market access should be considered alongside production when designing food-security interventions."
